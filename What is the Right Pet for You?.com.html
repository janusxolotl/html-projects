Traditional Domestic Pets

common domesticated animals that have lived alongside humans for generations as household companions.

Dogs
is a domesticated carnivorous mammal descended from the gray wolf, commonly kept by humans as a pet, working animal, or guard animal.
Cats
a small, domesticated carnivorous mammal (Felis catus) belonging to the family Felidae, or more broadly, any member of that same family including large wild cats like lions, tigers, and leopards
Rabbits
often known affectionately by the pet name bunny or bunny rabbit, especially when referring to young, domesticated rabbits, healthy indoor pet rabbits can live up to 10 years and longer.

Rodents or Small Pets

small, intelligent, and relatively low-maintenance companions that fit well into various living spaces, sometimes referred to as "pocket pets"

Hamsters
most common family pets, hamsters are easy to handle and tend to move slowly and tend to be nocturnal but can be active for periods during the day.
Chinchillas
generally quiet, “hands-off” pets that do best when left alone to run about in their cage, may live up to 20 years with a proper diet and good healthcare and do not tolerate hot, humid environments.
Gerbils
are friendly and inquisitive, who are active, entertaining animals and love to climb, burrow, and chew. They make excellent pets for older children and teenagers, are desert animals, they have a very low water requirement.
Guinea Pigs
also known as cavies make good family pets, generally hardy, easy to care for, and make great family pets if they are handled frequently and gently. They rarely bite or scratch and are docile, non-aggressive, responsive animals.
Mice
popular, somewhat timid, social, intelligent, and entertaining pets, but may not be for everyone. Mice tend to be nocturnal but are active for short periods during the day, they do not shed a lot are typically small more fragile than other rodents.
Rats
are extremely intelligent, inquisitive, and social pets and can be trained to come on command and to perform various tricks.

Birds

any avian species (excluding domestic poultry) kept in captivity for human companionship, pleasure, or entertainment

Parakeets
Small, colorful, friendly and great for beginners, live about 5 to 13 years, usually learn to mimic words.
Cockatiels
Affectionate and gentle with a distinctive crest; lives up to 16 to 30 years and enjoy whistling or singing.
Lovebirds
Energetic and social small parrots that require dedicated daily attention.
Canaries
Quiet, independent watch-only birds, known for male singing
Finches
Independent and quiet birds that prefer to live in small groups
Pigeons
Gentle and quiet alternatives to parrots.
Large Parrots
Extremely intelligent and social, require hours of out-of-cage time daily, and can live for decades and are not recommended for first-time owners

Aquatic Pets

popular, quiet, and low-maintenance pets that require an established, filtered aquarium system rather than just a simple bowl

Betta Fish
Solitary and colorful fishes, where males must be kept alone and requires at least a 5-gallon heated and filtered tank.
Guppies
Peaceful schooling and active fish that do well in community freshwater setups.
Mollies
Adaptable and live-bearing fishes that add bright colors to a tank, can be conjoined with other types of fishes.

Reptiles

a cold-blooded, air-breathing vertebrate animal belonging to the class Reptilia, characterized by dry skin covered in scales or bony plates and typically laying soft-shelled eggs on land.

Lizards
require specialized care, including temperature and humidity control, UVB lighting, and species-specific diets, while their attitude and temperament range from calm and docile to shy or flighty depending on the species
Snakes
generally calm, low-maintenance reptiles that require specific temperature control and a diet of pre-killed rodents.
Turtles
long-term, low-interaction exotic pets that require specialized aquatic or land enclosures, strict lighting, and regular cleaning rather than cuddling.

Insects

require minimal physical space, produce odorless and dry waste, do not need daily walks, and offer a low-cost quiet alternative to traditional vertebrate pets.

Herbivorous
rely entirely on plants for food, they do not possess the venomous stings or aggressive hunting instincts found in carnivorous invertebrates. Their "attitude" is characterized by extreme calmness, relying heavily on camouflage rather than aggression.
Predatory
low-maintenance pets, but they require a steady supply of live prey and a "hands-off" approach to handling; they also have specialized housing requirements to prevent cannibalism and facilitate hunting.
